# Introduction

Depression and anxiety are highly prevalent mental disorders with substantial clinical, social, and economic consequences, and they remain a persistent challenge for clinical practice and public health research. Despite advances in psychotherapy and pharmacotherapy, a significant proportion of patients exhibit incomplete response, relapse, or therapeutic resistance, which has driven the search for novel intervention strategies. In this context, psilocybin has gained relevance as a compound of growing pharmacological and clinical interest, particularly in relation to its potential application in patients with refractory depression and anxiety. Recent literature has suggested possible therapeutic effects associated with its administration in clinically supervised settings and with psychological support; however, the evidence remains limited, heterogeneous, and subject to important methodological differences.

The development of this research line is grounded in a broader set of historical and scientific antecedents, ranging from psychedelic research, the study of serotonergic mechanisms, and the evaluation of alternative therapeutic interventions for mental health, to the contemporary resurgence of assisted and structured therapeutic models. The accumulated evidence on psilocybin suggests that the compound may modulate affective experience, cognitive perception, and emotional regulation, factors that are central to the treatment of depressive and anxious disorders. However, the diversity of protocols, populations studied, outcome measures, and follow-up periods requires a methodologically rigorous synthesis capable of integrating clinical, pharmacological, and bibliographic evidence in a coherent and reproducible manner. In this sense, the systematic review represents an appropriate tool for evaluating the current state of the field and for identifying scientific gaps and opportunities for future research.

The legislative and regulatory dimension is an essential component in the construction of knowledge about psilocybin. In different jurisdictions, the compound is subject to strict legal restrictions, controlled clinical research frameworks, and specific regulatory mechanisms. This normative variability directly affects the feasibility of clinical trials, the availability of public evidence, the ethical design of protocols, and the implementation of therapeutic interventions. Moreover, the legal context not only defines the research framework, but also conditions the social and clinical interpretation of the compound, especially in a field where the debate around safety, therapeutic authorization, and health regulation remains highly relevant. For this reason, the systematic review should incorporate the legislative dimension as part of the contextual framework in which evidence is generated.

From a pharmacological perspective, bibliometric analysis is particularly useful for understanding the evolution of research on psilocybin in relation to depression and anxiety. Bibliometrics allows for identifying patterns in scientific production, influential authors and research groups, relevant publication sources, countries and international collaborations, as well as emerging terms and themes in the literature. In pharmacology and mental health, this type of analysis complements the systematic review by providing a structural description of the documentary corpus and by enabling the recognition of areas of growth, convergence, or fragmentation in the evidence. Likewise, bibliometric analysis helps situate psilocybin research within the broader landscape of psychedelic and therapeutic pharmacology, facilitating the identification of methodological gaps, research foci, and needs for clinical validation.

Based on these antecedents, the present study proposes to conduct a systematic review of the scientific literature on psilocybin, depression, and anxiety, following the reporting guidelines established by the PRISMA 2020 statement. The general objective is to identify, select, and synthesize the available evidence on the role of psilocybin in the treatment of these conditions, with particular attention to its clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory relevance. As specific objectives, the study aims to describe the scientific production on psilocybin through bibliometric analysis, examine the main themes and research trends, and evaluate the methodological quality and evidence level of the studies included. In addition, the review will follow a structured flow that includes identification of records, duplicate removal, title and abstract screening, eligibility assessment, and data extraction, in accordance with the transparency, reproducibility, and reporting standards proposed by PRISMA 2020.

The methodological justification for the present work lies in the need to provide a rigorous and transparent synthesis of a literature that is expanding rapidly but remains characterized by marked diversity in study design and regulatory contexts. The combination of systematic review and bibliometric analysis allows for a complementary examination of both the quality of the evidence and the structure and evolution of scientific production. This approach responds to the contemporary demand for systematic reviews that not only describe clinical findings, but also document the trajectory of a scientific field, its epistemological development, and its relationship with the pharmacological and legislative frameworks. Therefore, the review is presented as a methodologically solid contribution to understanding the role of psilocybin in depression and anxiety research.
